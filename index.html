ChordPlayer

future device · firmware fw-2026-04-07 · Main_ZB case (May 2026) · Raspberry Pi Pico

Seven keys in a zigzag play the seven chords of the scale. The encoders pick the root, mode and octave, and Markov chains suggest which chord comes next.

Encoders: drag, use the scroll wheel or the arrow keys; a click presses the shaft, and holding it for 1 s opens the settings (Base moves through them, Mode changes the value). Modifiers (Mod 1–3): hold one and play a chord key. Keys: click, tap (several at once) or use the computer keyboard.

History

    Main screen

    On the device: Settings → Screen, changed with the Mode encoder. DrumMode always shows the pads.

    Modifiers

    Note: hold it while you press a chord key and only the first note sounds; after that, each press of Note plays the next one (and starts over). Order picks the direction: up, down or random. If the chord is already sounding in full, it keeps sounding and Note replays its notes one by one on top. In Gate each note lasts as long as you hold Note; in Sustain they build up. In both, releasing the chord key stops them all. On the device: Settings → Note and Note order.

    Hold a modifier and play a chord key; they can be combined. On the device they are picked in Settings: Base down to Mod 1–3 and Mode to change it. 7th, add9 and add6 use the notes of the mode.

    Arpeggio and tempo

    120 BPM

    A step of length 1 lasts one beat (a quarter note) at this tempo. On the device they are in Settings (hold an encoder for 1 s): Base moves through the rows, Mode changes Arp, Tempo or Pattern, and a press on Pattern opens the editor. In the editor, Base picks the step, Mode its note and Octave its length.

    Sound

    audio waiting

    The device makes no sound itself: it sends MIDI over USB. Here a synthesizer in the browser plays the part of the DAW.

    MIDI output

    disconnected

    Channel 1 for chords and channel 10 in DrumMode. Web MIDI needs Chrome or Edge and a page on https or localhost.

    Real or proposed

    • From the firmware: root, mode and octave encoders; polyphonic keys; in DrumMode, turning Octave while holding a pad changes its note.
    • From the CAD: Main_ZB case, Key_V1_Long keys in a zigzag every 20 mm, 2.42" OLED, 3 encoders in a column and 3 buttons under the screen.
    • Proposed: the Chord_AI Markov chains on the device, genre on the Mode encoder's push switch, LEDs under the keys and bars on the OLED.
    • Proposed: no 7 "7th" modes from the firmware: the seventh is played with the 7th modifier.
    • Proposed: 6 more scales after the 7 modes: harmonic minor, melodic minor, Phrygian dominant, major and minor pentatonic, and blues.
    • Proposed: a Note button next to the modifiers (no hole in the CAD yet): a hand-played arpeggio, one note per press.
    • Proposed: the 3 buttons under the screen are chord modifiers. They are held before playing the key, and each one does whatever Settings says (seventh, ninth, reduced…).
    • Proposed: settings by holding any encoder for 1 s: Arp, Tempo, Pattern, New pattern, Mod 1–3, Note, Note order, Screen (chords or keyboard on the main screen). The Base encoder moves through the rows and Mode changes the value; a press only opens the editor, creates a pattern or exits. In the editor, Base picks the step, Mode changes its note (1–4 or rest) and Octave its length; a press on a step picks it up to move it. Save stores a preset (firmware presets are saved as a User N copy). Holding for 1 s again closes the menu without saving.
